# Research engine contract

This document describes the retained v0.1 Classic spot engine. The professional workspace's spot/perpetual, funding and out-of-sample contract is documented in [Professional research](pro-research.md).

Tidebench v0.1 simulates long-only spot positions from confirmed OHLCV bars. It is
a reproducible research model, not a reconstruction of an exchange matching
engine. No engine function connects to an exchange or submits a real order.

## Time and causality

`Candle.ts` is the UTC bar **opening** timestamp in milliseconds. Input must be
strictly ascending, unique, interval-aligned, confirmed, and contiguous. Prices
must be finite positive Decimals with valid OHLC bounds; volume must be finite
and non-negative. Invalid input is rejected without sorting, deduplicating,
interpolating, or silently discarding rows.

1. Execute an order generated by the previous closed bar at this bar's open.
2. Mark the portfolio at this bar's close.
3. Update indicators with this close and determine the next order intent.

Trade timestamps are execution opens. Equity timestamps are bar closes
(`Candle.ts + interval_ms`). A final-bar signal remains unfilled; open inventory
is marked at the final close without an invented liquidation. A prefix replay
must reproduce every earlier equity observation and fill. Changing future bars
must not change earlier decisions.

This matches the next-open convention used by
[backtesting.py](https://kernc.github.io/backtesting.py/doc/backtesting/backtesting.html).
The need to compare complete and sliced histories is also documented in
[Freqtrade's lookahead analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/lookahead-analysis/).

## Signals and sizing

- `sma_cross`: long when the fast SMA exceeds the slow SMA; otherwise flat.
  Wait for `slow` closes before deciding.
- `rsi_reversion`: Wilder RSI with an arithmetic initial average. Enter strictly
  below `entry`; exit strictly above `exit`; retain inventory between thresholds.
  Wait for `rsi_period + 1` closes. An unchanged series has RSI 50.
- `buy_hold`: enter on the first available closed bar's signal.

A transition from flat to long spends at most `allocation` of the available cash,
including its simulated fee. Repeated long signals retain the existing quantity;
there is no continuous rebalancing or pyramiding. A transition to flat sells all
inventory. Zero allocation is valid and remains flat.

## Execution and accounting

The price model is `next_open * (1 + slippage_bps / 10000)` for a buy and
`next_open * (1 - slippage_bps / 10000)` for a sell. Buy prices round upward to the
instrument tick; sell prices round downward. Quantities round down to the lot
size. Buys below the minimum size are skipped and counted in the assumptions.

Every fill pays `quantity * price * fee_bps / 10000` in the quote currency. Entry
cost basis includes the buy fee; realized PnL includes the sell fee. Cash and
inventory must remain non-negative. Decimal arithmetic is retained for prices,
quantities, balances and fees; JSON represents these values as decimal strings.

The arithmetic contract is an explicit **50 significant digits**, half-even Decimal context, independent of the caller's precision, rounding, exponent limits and traps. Prices, executable quantities, ledger amounts and equity must have absolute values **below `1e30`**; inputs may not exceed 50 significant digits. Unsupported step resolution, a non-progressing lot correction or out-of-domain compounding raises `EngineError` instead of returning an unresolvable ledger. The result's `assumptions.accounting` records these limits. This is finite decimal arithmetic, not an unlimited-precision claim.

The buy-and-hold benchmark enters with the full starting cash at the second bar's
open using the same price, lot, minimum-size and fee policies. Strategy allocation
may differ, so a return comparison is not a claim of risk-adjusted alpha.

## Metrics

Equity is `cash + inventory * close`. Drawdown uses the initial capital as part of
the high-water mark. Total return includes open-position mark-to-market. Trade
count means filled orders, not round trips. Fees reported in metrics are the
strategy's fees, not the benchmark's.

Sharpe uses only full UTC days and daily close-to-close equity returns, sample
standard deviation, zero assumed risk-free rate, and `sqrt(365)` annualization.
Fewer than 30 daily returns yields `null / insufficient_sample`; zero or
negligible variation yields `null / zero_variance`. The 30-return policy prevents
obviously misleading short-window displays. It does not establish statistical
significance or independent observations. Unsupported bar intervals cannot
produce a daily Sharpe.

Return, drawdown and Sharpe are descriptive statistics. Research involving many
parameter trials must account for selection and non-normal returns; see the
original [Probability of Backtest Overfitting paper](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)
and [Deflated Sharpe Ratio paper](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf).

## Boundaries and reproducibility

The model has no bid/ask book, queue priority, partial fills, market impact,
latency, intrabar stops, borrowing, funding, margin, or liquidation. A fixed
quote-currency fee is a simulation assumption, not a claim about every OKX fill.
OHLCV cannot identify the intrabar price path, a limitation also explained in
[Freqtrade's backtesting documentation](https://docs.freqtrade.io/en/latest/backtesting/).

The surrounding API must record data source and acquisition timestamps, dataset
identity, strategy/configuration, instrument metadata, engine version and result
artifacts. Changing that metadata must create a distinguishable research run.
Tests cover next-open execution, final-bar behavior, causal prefix invariance,
fees and initial drawdown, rounding, invalid data, UTC sample boundaries, and
random-path accounting solvency.
